# National Food Security Act 2013 (summary)

This is a simplified project summary. Check the official Act and your state's orders before relying on it.

## Purpose and coverage
The National Food Security Act, 2013 (NFSA) gives eligible households a legal right to subsidised foodgrains through the Public Distribution System (PDS). The Act covers up to 75 percent of the rural population and up to 50 percent of the urban population. State governments identify the eligible households.

## Household categories and monthly quantity
Priority households (PHH) are entitled to 5 kg of foodgrains per person per month. Antyodaya Anna Yojana (AAY) households, the poorest of the poor, are entitled to 35 kg per household per month regardless of family size. In this system the monthly quota is split between rice and wheat using a configurable rice share.

## Ration cards
The eldest woman of the household, aged 18 or above, is treated as the head of the household for the purpose of issuing the ration card. The card lists the family members. A card that is marked not eligible cannot receive grain until an officer reactivates it.

## Maternity and child benefits
Pregnant women and lactating mothers are entitled to a nutritious meal and a maternity benefit of not less than Rs 6,000 paid in instalments. Children aged 6 months to 14 years are entitled to age-appropriate meals through nutrition schemes.

## Grievance redressal
Every district has a District Grievance Redressal Officer and every state has a State Food Commission. If the entitled foodgrains are not supplied, the person may be entitled to a food security allowance as set by the state.
